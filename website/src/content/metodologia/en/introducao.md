The **Structured Document Evolution Methodology (MEDE)** was created to solve the chronic decay of software engineering documentation and elevate technical docs to high-quality governance assets.

## The Meaning Behind "MEDE"

The acronym **MEDE** carries an intentional double meaning in Portuguese, associating directly with the verb **medir (to measure)**. 

The methodology is not just about organizing text files; its scientific purpose is to establish clear metrics and boundaries over system knowledge. With MEDE, we can **measure**:

* **The Understanding Gap:** The exact difference between the initial business understanding (what was requested) and the current project state (what was actually built).
* **Knowledge Maturity:** The level of maturity and clarity of the business rules mapped to the solution.
* **New Rule Impact:** Assessing with precision how introducing a new functional rule affects pre-existing data models, schedules, and scope.

## "Selling the Idea": Is it worth the effort?

Writing structured documentation and executing rigorous transactional cycles demands effort. However, in mid-to-long-term projects, MEDE pays off quickly by offering competitive business advantages:

### 1. Seamless Team Onboarding

When developers join or leave a project, knowledge loss is usually devastating. With MEDE, the causal history and the consolidated state document (Current State) are so clear that the learning curve for new members is drastically shortened.

### 2. Easy Reimplementation & Refactoring

If in the future it is decided to migrate the technology stack (e.g., rewrite from Node.js to Go), the team won't need to decipher legacy code to understand business rules. The entire mapping of the software is already consolidated and validated in the live documents.

### 3. Fault-Proof Traceability

You will know exactly who decided to change a specific data rule, when, and why, backed by an immutable causal link.
